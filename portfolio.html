RowPal

iOS & watchOS In final testing

RowPal is an iOS rowing app. It works with any rowing machine and allows rowers to race their previous best performances or a pace boat.

The project started with a proof of concept to see which sensors would be the most accurate to measure rowing motion, the front-facing camera or the accelerometer of the Apple Watch. After lots of testing, including thermal and battery tests, the front camera was the clear winner, able to track full rowing strokes over long durations with almost 100% accuracy.

With the concept proven, I began the design process. Some attributes simply can’t be inferred from a camera (stroke power being the big one) so I decided early that this would not be a pro rowing app, but a fun consumer-level product to encourage daily movement. Think Wii Sports. That decision informed a more playful visual design that manages the user’s expectations, rather than a dashboard of splits and watts etc.

I did want some level of accuracy though. The app’s “virtual metres” are calibrated against real datasets — Olympic results, club races, and rowing biomechanics literature — so a stroke in RowPal moves you about as far as it would on the water. The boat itself runs on real hull physics: stroke impulse, quadratic drag, the surge-and-glide of an actual shell.

Look Up

iOS & watchOS Live on the App Store

The International Space Station is brightest at dusk and dawn as it moves across the sky, but it’s easy to miss. Unless you know where to look. Look Up tells you when it will be passing and how to locate it.

The live compass shows the next visible pass. Rotate your phone for sky mode to pinpoint the station’s location as it crosses the sky. A live globe view displays its current position, the area it’s visible from, and its heading. With a dedicated Apple Watch app, notifications, and home screen widgets, you should never miss a pass (weather permitting).
